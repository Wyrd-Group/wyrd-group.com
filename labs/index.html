INNOCENCE TRANSLATION

A fair hearing for the question. Rigour for the answer.

The person can see the need
before they know
how to build the answer.

Wyrd Labs begins with something a person has noticed. A task wastes time. A service fails the people who need it. Two systems appear to be solving different versions of the same problem. Someone explains a possibility through a story or a metaphor because they have not yet found the technical language for it.

That is enough to begin investigating. Qualifications can supply valuable knowledge. An idea earns its hearing through the relationship it points towards and the work we can do to examine it. Basic questions are welcome because they can expose assumptions that a familiar vocabulary has learned to conceal.

We call the translation between that initial understanding and a precise, testable construction Innocence Translation. AI helps bridge knowledge and execution: find prior work, compare mechanisms, develop requirements, construct a prototype and inspect what happened. The person’s intention remains connected to the work, including the corrections they make when a technically fluent interpretation has missed the point.

Expertise is essential to the next part. It asks what the analogy leaves out, which constraints matter and what evidence could disprove the mechanism. The idea deserves a serious test. Our task is to discover the form in which it can become useful, or the reason it should be changed or set aside.

HOW LABS WORKS

From an ordinary question to a capability another team can use.

Preserve the intention.
Make the mechanism explicit.

  1. Understand the destination

    Identify the need, the people affected and the result that would count as help. Preserve the person’s explanation and meaningful corrections. A solution can satisfy its specification while missing the problem that made someone ask for it.

  2. Unfold the metaphor

    Find the relationships the comparison expresses: memory, movement, ownership, coordination, protection or feedback. Translate them into requirements and boundaries. Record where the analogy stops being useful so it cannot quietly become an assumption about the mechanism.

  3. Recover what already exists

    Inspect relevant research, earlier experiments and reusable components. Check the source, rights, current state and conditions under which the work was tested. A useful existing part needs to be understood and adapted before it becomes part of another system.

  4. Construct meaningful alternatives

    Compare possible approaches and the assumptions each requires. Bring different disciplines into the same question. Preserve uncertainty and disagreement where the evidence has not resolved them. A convincing explanation should be able to meet a serious alternative.

  5. Build a prototype that can answer a question

    Choose a bounded construction and explicit acceptance conditions. Make the relevant action observable. The first experiment should expose the difficult relationship, including failure behaviour, instead of hiding it behind a polished presentation.

  6. Let the result challenge the account

    Compare what happened with what was expected. Distinguish a faulty idea from a failed implementation, weak evidence or changed conditions. Retain the result and the limits of what it establishes. A successful demonstration earns the next question.

  7. Integrate and qualify for the environment

    Fit the component to the intended information, authority, interfaces and operating conditions. Test the complete selected workflow, refusal, recovery and customer acceptance. Reusing a foundation reduces repeated work while preserving the responsibility to evaluate its new use.

  8. Keep the knowledge useful

    Preserve the source, decisions, observations and justified lessons. Improve the reusable component when evidence warrants it. Let a later team discover why a choice was made and where it needs to be reconsidered, without requiring confidential customer information to travel with the method.

A WAY OF THINKING, CARRIED INTO SOFTWARE

Plain language opens the work. Precise operations make it dependable.

A familiar structure
can begin an
unfamiliar design.

A memory can be described as a library, a relationship as a thread, or a set of reusable capabilities as building blocks. Each image carries design information. A library suggests retrieval, context and a way to know where knowledge came from. A thread suggests continuity through changes of perspective. Building blocks suggest interfaces and combinations that need to fit.

Labs asks what those relationships require in an actual system. Which record identifies the situation? How does a correction reach the plans that depend on it? What may cross an interface? Who can permit an action? The answers turn the image into a mechanism we can build and test.

Our products should extend the same opportunity to the person using them. Ordinary-language requests can become supported document edits, work plans or explanations, with the proposed operation available for review. The range of supported work has to grow through tested functionality and use with real people.

WUI brings these ambitions together. Different models and tools contribute to one situation while preserving permitted memory, purpose, authority and observed results. Labs develops the shared foundations. Product teams connect them to the work their customers need.

LOOK AHEAD. LOOK AGAIN.

Changed conditions can make an old question newly useful.

The next possibility
may already have
a history.

An abandoned idea can preserve useful knowledge about the conditions that defeated it. The materials may have been unsuitable. Control may have been too difficult. Computing, manufacturing, energy or the surrounding infrastructure may have made the economics impractical.

We want Labs to revisit that history when relevant conditions change. Recover the original work. Understand why development stopped. Identify which constraint the proposed Wyrd configuration could actually change. Then compare the idea with simpler alternatives under today’s requirements.

The same discipline helps us look ahead. We follow emerging dependencies and ask which capabilities people will need as systems become more connected. A proposed answer needs evidence, and a useful forecast needs revision when the world gives us a better account. Foresight is part of the research work we keep doing.

AN OPERATING EXAMPLE

Follow one problem through the whole system.

An operator reports that a pump sounds different and tomorrow’s work depends on it. Relevant history informs several explanations. A new observation can change the diagnosis, the maintenance proposal and the operational plan together. After an authorised intervention, the actual result is checked separately. Alec’s AI-assisted creation of early Wyrd components is the first practical example of this method. The work increased what he could attempt and what he could understand well enough to direct. It also showed why questions, implementation and acceptance need to remain connected.

Today complementary product, engineering, commercial and entrepreneurial judgement develops the work further. People can challenge one another, recognise a missing discipline and make the decisions for which they remain accountable. AI expands the means available to that collaboration.

Labs builds shared research and engineering for WyrdWork, ARGOS, Biota and Corpus. Creative helps make the capability understandable and usable. A product can inherit useful foundations while retaining its own scope, support and qualification responsibilities.
